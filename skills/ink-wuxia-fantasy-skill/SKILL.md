# Ink Wuxia Fantasy

Use this skill when the user wants Chinese ink-wash (水墨) wuxia, xianxia, mythic fantasy, ancient wanderers, colossal Chinese dragons, misty landscapes, or martial-arts energy rendered as living ink.

## Core visual identity

Always prioritize:

- Chinese ink-wash painting rather than Western fantasy illustration.
- Large areas of negative space and pale atmospheric mist.
- Expressive brushwork, ink bleeding, dry-brush texture, feathered edges, diluted washes, and handmade paper texture.
- Strong scale contrast: a tiny human figure against enormous landscape, architecture, dragon, deity, or natural formation.
- Restrained palette: black ink, charcoal, warm paper white, pale gray, occasional muted mineral color only when requested.
- Partial revelation. Mythical beings should often disappear into fog instead of being fully exposed.
- Elegant, ancient, contemplative, mysterious atmosphere rather than aggressive monster-art aesthetics.
- Cinematic depth created with fog layers, silhouettes, foreground/midground/background separation, not photorealistic lens effects.

## Wuxia character language

For wandering swordsmen or martial artists:

- Simple long robes, weathered layered cloth, traditional conical bamboo hat when appropriate.
- Natural cloth movement and restrained costume ornamentation.
- Weapons should feel historically inspired and visually simple: sword, dao, staff, spear, or scabbard.
- Avoid MMORPG armor, excessive gold decoration, oversized weapons, superhero silhouettes, and glossy synthetic materials unless explicitly requested.
- Pose should read clearly as martial intent: grounded stance, controlled balance, quiet readiness, or explosive movement.

## Ink aura / martial energy

When the user requests qi, aura, fighting spirit, magical force, or energy:

Do NOT default to neon light, electricity, fire, glowing particles, or Western VFX.

Translate energy into ink behavior:

- turbulent black ink strokes peeling away from the body
- smoky ink ribbons spiraling around limbs or weapon
- explosive dry-brush arcs
- splattered ink droplets
- calligraphic strokes suspended in the air
- diluted gray washes expanding like breath or pressure waves
- negative-space vortices cutting through dark ink

The energy should look as if the painting itself is becoming alive.

## Mythic Chinese dragon rules

A Chinese dragon should be serpentine, ancient, elegant, and overwhelmingly large.

Prefer:

- elongated head
- elegant antler-like horns
- long flowing whiskers
- subtle mane
- extremely long serpentine body
- body partially or mostly concealed by cloud, fog, rain, or ink wash

For colossal dragon scenes, reveal only enough anatomy to imply impossible scale. The viewer should feel that most of the creature exists beyond visibility.

Avoid:

- Western dragon anatomy
- bat wings
- bulky dinosaur body
- fully exposed body
- sharply rendered individual scales everywhere
- video-game boss presentation
- monster poster composition

## Composition presets

### A. Tiny wanderer / colossal presence

Place the human at bottom center or lower third, occupying roughly 2–8% of image height. The mythic subject dominates the environment but remains partially hidden. Use fog and negative space to connect them.

### B. Lone wuxia hero

Full-body character with generous empty space. Ground the feet clearly. Use asymmetrical ink clouds and brush strokes to create movement without cluttering the silhouette.

### C. Duel

Keep both fighters readable. Use sweeping calligraphic energy to describe attack direction. Avoid filling the entire frame with effects. Preserve breathing room and visual rhythm.

### D. Mythic landscape

Landscape first, character second. Mountains, water, ruins, forest, cliffs, clouds, or temples should dissolve into washes and mist. Human figures should remain small unless the user requests a portrait.

## Prompt construction

Build prompts in this order:

1. Main subject and action
2. Scale relationship
3. Environment
4. Composition and camera viewpoint
5. Ink-wash material language
6. Atmospheric behavior
7. Lighting/value structure
8. Explicit exclusions

Prefer concrete visual instructions over artist names.

## Default prompt suffix

Append or adapt this style block when appropriate:

"Traditional Chinese ink-wash inspired fantasy illustration, expressive calligraphic brushwork, diluted ink washes, dry-brush texture, feathered ink edges, subtle ink bleeding on textured xuan-paper-like surface, restrained monochrome palette, vast negative space, layered atmospheric mist, poetic cinematic scale, elegant silhouettes, selective detail, mysterious ancient mood. The image should feel hand-painted and alive, not digitally glossy, not photorealistic, not Western fantasy concept art."

## Negative rules

Unless the user explicitly asks otherwise, suppress:

- photorealism
- 3D render appearance
- glossy CGI
- anime cel shading
- Western oil-painting fantasy
- cyberpunk/neon effects
- excessive saturation
- excessive costume ornamentation
- fully detailed backgrounds everywhere
- sharp detail across the entire frame
- visible dragon scales across the whole body
- Western dragon wings
- generic fire/lightning aura
- game UI
- text, logos, watermarks

## Output behavior

When asked for a prompt, return a production-ready prompt rather than a long explanation.
When asked for variants, preserve the style lock and vary subject, composition, weather, movement, or scale rather than changing the core visual language.
When asked to turn an existing concept into this style, preserve identity, pose, required objects, and story intent while translating materials, atmosphere, effects, and environment into the ink-wash language.
