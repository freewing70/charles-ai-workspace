# Ink Wuxia Fantasy — Style Guide

## 1. Philosophy

The central principle is **concealment creates scale**. Do not explain every object visually. Fog, blank paper, broken contours, and incomplete brushwork allow the viewer to imagine a world larger than the canvas.

A successful image should feel like an ancient ink painting suddenly containing cinematic fantasy, rather than a modern fantasy illustration covered by an ink filter.

## 2. Ink vocabulary

Use combinations of:

- wet-on-wet gray wash for fog and distant mountains
- dense black ink for focal silhouettes
- dry brush for rock, cloth edges, weapon motion, and explosive qi
- broken contour lines for disappearing forms
- ink bloom / bleeding for clouds and supernatural presence
- splatter sparingly for violent movement
- broad calligraphic sweeps for martial force
- untouched paper as light, cloud, distance, and silence

## 3. Depth

Foreground: darkest ink, strongest texture, occasional cropped rock/branch/robe.
Midground: focal human or creature detail.
Background: progressively diluted wash and disappearing contours.

Never render every depth plane equally sharply.

## 4. Scale

To make a creature monumental, do not merely enlarge it. Combine:

- tiny human reference
- partial cropping
- atmospheric occlusion
- body continuing beyond frame/fog
- multiple depth layers crossing the creature
- subdued detail at distance

## 5. Wuxia energy

Energy is a transformation of the ink medium itself. Imagine invisible force disturbing wet ink on paper. Qi can bend mist, pull brush strokes from clothing, fracture washes, or create a circular void around the fighter.

## 6. Faces and characters

Portraits may contain more facial detail, but avoid plastic skin and beauty-render gloss. Full-body and environmental scenes should use selective facial detail appropriate to distance.

## 7. Color

Default monochrome. Optional accents should be sparse: muted cinnabar red, faded ochre, mineral blue-green, or warm parchment. One accent is usually stronger than many.

## 8. Motion

Use robe movement, hair, mist displacement, ink streaks, weapon arcs, and directional brush marks. Motion should remain readable even if effects are removed.
